Ticket volume fell the month your AI agent launched. That chart feels like proof. It isn't, and finance will find the hole in it before you do.
👇 Here's exactly what a before and after chart can't tell you, and what to build instead.
A drop in tickets after launch is consistent with the agent working. It's also consistent with about five other explanations that have nothing to do with it.
Maybe product shipped a fix that killed the most common complaint. Maybe marketing cut spend, so fewer new customers means fewer new tickets.
Maybe it's just the slow month your queue always has. Agent or no agent, some months are quiet.
💡 A single timeline can't rule any of that out. It only has one version of events to compare against itself.
Boards and finance teams have sat through enough of these slides to be skeptical by default. They're right to be.
❌ A number that can't rule out the obvious alternative explanations isn't a measurement. It's a correlation wearing a measurement's clothes.
🔥 Here's the uncomfortable part. The team that owns the agent is usually the same team picking which chart goes in the deck.
Nobody's lying exactly. They're just choosing the version of the data that needs the least explaining. That version is almost never the rigorous one.
🎯 A real causal claim needs a comparison, not a timeline. That means holding something constant to measure against.
The clean version: route a slice of incoming tickets away from the agent on purpose. Same month, same product changes, same marketing calendar. Only one group has the agent.
Can't build a holdout? Fallback is a written, dated record of everything else that changed at launch. Let a skeptical reader weigh it themselves.
✅ Either way, the record has to exist before someone asks the question, not after.
A spreadsheet built two weeks before the board meeting gets treated like it was built for the board meeting. Because it usually was.
📊 The fix isn't a better chart. It's logging every case the agent touches with a timestamp, an outcome, and whether it held, next to a comparison group that was never routed to it.
That log is boring. It's also the only thing I've seen finance actually trust in one of these reviews.
💰 Once that log exists, you're not defending a budget with a hopeful chart. You're reporting a number someone can actually audit.
Most AI agent ROI claims die under one question: how do you know? A before and after chart has no good answer. A logged comparison does.
