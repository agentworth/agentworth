FAQ

What counts as a resolution for ROI purposes?

Not every closed ticket is a resolution, and the gap between the two is where a lot of ROI numbers quietly fall apart.

Short answer

A resolution, for ROI purposes, should mean the customer's actual issue was solved and stayed solved, not that a ticket status changed to closed. Those are two different things, and a system that only tracks the second one will always report a higher number than the first one would support.

Why the difference matters

A ticket can be closed for reasons that have nothing to do with the issue being fixed. A customer gives up and stops responding, an agent closes a case to clear a queue, or a conversation times out after a set number of hours. None of those are resolutions in any meaningful sense, but all of them can inflate a reported resolution count if the system counts by status rather than by outcome.

A tighter definition checks whether the same issue comes back within a reasonable window. If it does, the original closure was not really a resolution regardless of what the status field said, and counting it as one overstates what the agent actually accomplished.
