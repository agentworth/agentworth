FAQ

Why does my AI agent show a high resolution rate but a bad ROI number?

Resolution rate measures activity. ROI measures what that activity was actually worth once every cost is counted.

Short answer

A high resolution rate only counts how many conversations ended in a closed status. It says nothing about the cost of running the agent, the labor still spent reviewing escalations, or whether those resolutions actually held. An agent can close almost everything it touches and still produce a poor ROI number if what it costs to run, directly and in the human time still needed around it, outweighs the value of what it resolved.

Where the gap usually comes from

The most common cause is a cost side that got underweighted when the resolution rate was first reported. Model or platform usage costs that scale with volume, the time a human spends reviewing flagged or escalated cases, and the cost of resolutions that quietly came back as repeat contacts all belong on the cost side of the ledger, and a program that only tracks resolution rate usually has not been counting all three.

The fix is not to distrust the resolution rate itself, it is to stop treating it as a stand in for ROI. The two numbers answer different questions, one about how much the agent is doing and one about whether that activity is actually worth more than it costs, and a team that only tracks the first one has no way to notice when the second one turns negative.
